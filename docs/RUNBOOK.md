# The organizers' runbook

This is the Hack the Change 2026 external runbook from Code the Change YYC, copied out as text so every session can read it. The PDF itself stays out of the repo because it lists organizers' personal emails and this repo is public. For logistics questions, use **#ask-organizers** on Discord. If the runbook and this file ever disagree, the runbook wins, and Discord **#announcements** overrides both.

What these rules mean for Amble, and how we plan to score, is in [JUDGING.md](JUDGING.md).

## Rules that can disqualify us

- **Team:** 2 to 5 registered members. Everyone checks in in person.
- **Made here:** all design, code, Git repos and hardware are made during the hackathon. Libraries, frameworks and open-source tools are fine. Figma-only submissions aren't allowed, and neither are reskins of existing AI tools.
- **AI use must be disclosed on Devpost.** Not disclosing it can mean disqualification and a report to MLH.
- **"An inability to explain any technical design and/or code during judging may result in disqualification."** Every one of us has to be able to explain any part of it. See [WALKTHROUGH.md](WALKTHROUGH.md).
- **All members attend judging in person.** No remote presenting.
- **Teams work independently.** No sharing code between teams. Mentors can advise but can't write code for us.
- **Time limits are hard.** "Be on time and stay within your allotted presentation time. No exceptions."

## Submission

- On **Devpost by 11:30 AM ABT, Sunday Nov 8, SHARP.** Late or incomplete submissions are not accepted.
- **Title format:** `TEAM ID - TEAM NAME - PROJECT NAME`, so ours is `<team id> - <team name> - Amble`.
- **Must include:** a project description, the team members, a **2 to 5 minute video**, and a **link to the GitHub repo**, plus the AI disclosure.
- It must be accessible to judges: public repo, public video, working links.

## Prescreening (Round 0, results by 12:30 PM Sunday)

Fail any of these and we don't reach live judging. "NO EXCEPTIONS."

1. Submitted on time.
2. Title in the right format.
3. Has the 2 to 5 minute video and the GitHub link.
4. Passes every row of this pass/fail check, and "appears complete":

| Category | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Adherence to theme | Aligns with the theme and the specific challenge statement |
| Functionality | Demonstrates basic functionality and can be executed or run as intended |
| Innovation | Shows a creative approach to solving the problem |
| Completeness | Complete, and includes all required components (code, presentation, demo) |
| Technical feasibility | Technically feasible, with appropriate use of technology |
| Presentation | Presented clearly and effectively, with explanations of goals and outcomes |

The scoring rubric also says: "Groups have to be at LEAST satisfactory in the IDEA and EFFECTIVENESS category to move on to the judges." Satisfactory is 3 out of 5. The rubric has no rows literally called Idea or Effectiveness, so read Idea as Innovation plus Impact, and Effectiveness as Technical Execution plus UX, and aim for 3 or more on all five.

## Live judging (Engineering Lounge, ENE 143, from 12:45 PM)

- **Round 1:** a random panel of 3 to 4 judges, each scoring on their own, averaged. Slot is 7 minutes: **3 min presentation, 2 min Q&A, 2 min changeover.** A CTC member times it and **cuts us off at 3 minutes**.
- **Round 2:** the same again with a **different** panel of 3 to 4 judges, back to back with Round 1. So we pitch twice, to people who haven't seen it.
- **Round 3, finals:** the top teams present to **all judges**. Slot is 10 minutes: **5 min presentation, 3 min Q&A, 2 min changeover**, with the same hard cutoff. Each judge's placing earns **3, 2 or 1 points** for 1st, 2nd and 3rd. The three teams with the most points win. **Ties are broken by the Round 1 rubric score**, so Round 1 matters twice.
- Slots may be longer if there are few teams, capped at 7 min in rounds 1 and 2 and 10 min in finals. The schedule is on hackthechangeyyc.ca and can change.
- All teams are automatically eligible for every side pot.

## The scoring rubric (each category 1 to 5)

The sheet says "Total score: out of 50". Five categories at 5 points each is 25, so they're probably weighted equally and doubled. Don't plan around any one row being worth more.

Note the wording: this rubric talks about **urban challenges** and making **cities more inclusive, safe, resilient and sustainable**. Judges will read it literally, so say how Amble does each of those.

| Category | What judges ask | 3, satisfactory | 5, excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | Is the idea original or a fresh take? Does it creatively apply technology to urban challenges (housing, mobility, disaster resilience, inclusivity)? | A clear creative twist on existing approaches. Technology used appropriately for an urban challenge | Highly original or a transformative rethinking. Technology applied in a creative, well-justified way that could inspire broader innovation |
| **Impact & Relevance to Prompt** | Does it clearly address making cities more inclusive, safe, resilient and sustainable? Could it meaningfully improve lives? | Directly targets at least one key area of the prompt and defines a clear problem. Realistic benefits for a specific community or group | Embodies the prompt and addresses inclusivity, safety, resilience and sustainability in a unified way. Significant, scalable impact with clear, measurable outcomes |
| **Feasibility & Scalability** | Is it practical given real constraints (budget, infrastructure)? Could it scale beyond a demo into a real city or community? | Practical plan that takes real constraints into account. Could evolve into a pilot or early community project with support | Highly executable within real-world limits, with full understanding of the constraints. Exceptional potential to scale into a real initiative |
| **Technical Execution** | Quality of the implementation (working prototype, depth, stability)? Appropriate stack? Technically sound? | Runs start to finish and demonstrates all core features. Appropriate stack, sound implementation | Strong technical depth and reliability, with clear potential to become a production-quality product. Stack and architecture chosen and implemented with precision |
| **User Experience & Design** | Intuitive, accessible, user-friendly? Aesthetically pleasing? Does the design elevate the function and the idea? | Layout and flow intuitive for most users, clear interactions. Clean visuals that complement the function | Seamless, engaging and universally accessible. Visually striking yet purposeful, encouraging adoption |

What 4 adds over 3: Innovation, "fresh and distinct". Impact, "connects strongly to multiple aspects of the prompt with tangible examples". Feasibility, "realistic budgeting, infrastructure alignment and a plan for adoption". Technical, "stable, well-integrated, smooth, decisions clear and well-justified". UX, "smooth and inclusive, attention to accessibility and small interaction details".

What scores 1 or 2: an unoriginal idea or shallow tech, an unclear impact, ignored costs or adoption, an incomplete or unstable prototype with missing functionality, and a confusing interface that lacks accessibility features.

## The prompt we answer

Prompt 1: "How can we leverage technology to promote health, social connection, and well-being in our communities?" Build a functional prototype addressing at least one focus area under it. Solutions should be "scalable, sustainable, and have the potential to make a positive impact on society."

1. **Community Connection & Inclusion:** reduce loneliness for vulnerable populations (seniors, newcomers, marginalized groups), and encourage neighbourhood engagement such as volunteering, skill-sharing and community building.
2. **Proactive Health & Wellness:** support mental, physical or holistic well-being through habit tracking, mindfulness or preventative care education, and help users manage stress, work-life balance, and financial and mental health.
3. **Healthcare Accessibility & Literacy:** telehealth or remote monitoring tools, and platforms that translate complex medical data. (Not ours.)

Prompt 2 is about skills and careers. Not ours.
